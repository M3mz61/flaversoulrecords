Chapter 5

The Ghost Contract & The High-Fidelity Override

The operational friction with Sleazy Deep reached its peak during the release cycle for my remix of Jizzy Spunk's 'It's Over.' The label's representation, managed via Rob Made, pushed forward with the release while failing to deliver a formal, executed contract for the audio master. Instead of a professional production framework, the label attempted to force a choice between two heavily compromised remix versions, even going so far as to label 'Something Nu' as a total failure to justify their internal curation deficiencies.

Despite their dismissive gatekeeping stance, the organic momentum of the tracks across the databases exposed the reality. They rushed uncredited masters into their commercial compilation bundles to capitalize on the studio low-end definition I had engineered, all while keeping the physical creator entirely uncompensated and uncredited on the retail store frontends. No paperwork was ever signed, meaning the label never legally acquired the transmission or exploitation rights for that specific audio mix-buss configuration.

The resolution to this contractual void is executed cleanly in 2026. Rather than relying on their broken legacy pipelines, the high-fidelity original remaster is being ingested directly via the Symphonic wholesale B2B partner tier. By issuing a completely independent ISRC identifier under the native GXL3V prefix, the master recording is structurally reclaimed. The old, uncredited aggregators lose their metadata leverage, allowing the pristine audio file to route directly to Traxsource and Beatport under absolute, independent label autonomy.
