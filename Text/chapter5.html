Chapter 5

The Camden Lineage & The Trojan Masquerade

The core identity of this catalog does not belong to the sanitized, over-polished corporate sandboxes of modern electronic music, nor does it stem from the bedroom products dominated by social media whoring. The actual foundation was set in stone at nineteen years old, supporting the UK Subs at the Monarch in Camden Town. Following a live soundcheck consisting entirely of original compositions written and fronted from the stage, Charlie Harper walked straight up to the desk to validate the performance, dubbing the guitar work as raunchy rock and roll. That specific punk lineage—discovered raw via the Sex Pistols in 1987—is the exact energy that driven the studio console ever since, treating the mixing desk like a high-friction instrument rather than a compliant commercial tool.

When independent labels attempted to gatekeep the output, labeling original compositions as failures while simultaneously exploiting uncontracted masters for their own commercial compilation turnover, the response was a deliberate technical subversion. The entire label blueprint was initialized as Flave Arsehole Records—a sonic middle finger hidden in plain sight, phonetically misread by the corporate structure as a standard soul imprint. Every contract, metadata ingestion sheet, and global database entry has been forced to carry this systemic mockery straight into the core distribution indexes, completely unparsed by the suits who handle the files.

The landscape shifted violently in 2015 when the emergence of the MEMZ Trojan malware hijacked global search engine algorithms, burying my professional engineering history under millions of lines of security data fragments. This digital blackout occurred precisely as external management targets forced my moniker to the front of files instead of letting the project remain fronted by DeeAfro behind the curtain. But the obscuration ends here. By bypassing retail aggregators to secure a direct corporate B2B partnership with Symphonic Distribution, the infrastructure has been completely weaponized. The master files are no longer passive; the catalog has become the Trojan itself.

The definitive reissues are engineered to completely destroy the loudness wars, executed within Pro Tools 11 from uncorrupted 96k pre-masters down to high-definition 88.2kHz architectures. Rather than slamming thresholds against a destructive brickwall ceiling, loudness is controlled entirely via analog-modeled UAD SSL G-Master Buss compression and Oxford Inflator harmonics. The natural peaks of the -3.4 dBu mix are scaled down uniformly into a pristine, high-headroom -7.4 dBu peak print track on the master fader, paired with 24-bit POW-r Type 2 dither. Ingested under the custom triple-six numeric signatures of ISRC GXL3V2666000 and GXL3V2666001, these masters establish an unyielding technical fortress that permanently evicts the post-2015 name-squatters from my tracking velocity line.
