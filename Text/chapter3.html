The 2004 Sealed Master & The House Distortion

The origins of the modern catalog do not belong to the electronic music pipelines or the digital distribution services that eventually stripped out the credits. The real foundation sits inside a sealed, post-marked paper parcel resting at my parents' house, dispatched via registered mail back in 2004. I had tracked, engineered, and packaged those original song iterations entirely by myself a year prior in 2003—acting as a raw rock guitarist operating within the urban youth landscape of North and East London long before the technology allowed aggregators to overwrite metadata fields at will.

A full decade later, those exact sovereign compositions were extracted from the archive to be reworked, accelerated, and remolded into deep house and nu-disco formats. When labels like Sleazy Deep ingested tracks like 'Something Nu' into their commercial pipelines, their systems executing the metadata delivery stripped out the engineering details and fabricated joint credit lines out of sheer procedural laziness. They left the production fields entirely blank and treated a distinct, sole-authored composition as a generic shared commodity across digital compilations like Ibiza Sleaze.

The paper trail, however, remains completely unyielding. The international databases at the PRS registry under CAE designation 1353280662 and ICE Work Key 118735430 match the physical, sealed 2004 postmark perfectly. The digital distribution companies can alter the tags on their backend panels to read whatever lazy configurations their scripts default to, but they cannot rewrite the physical chronological timeline of the master recording's creation.
